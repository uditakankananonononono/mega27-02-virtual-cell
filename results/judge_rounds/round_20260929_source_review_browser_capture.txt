Candid verdict: HOLD is correct

The M63 Keio branch is potentially scientifically worthwhile, but not yet a valid rescue or a sufficiently specified novelty test. The strongest defensible framing is not “Gerdes essentiality was wrong,” because the old Gerdes gate and the new Keio assay measure different things. Instead, the new experiment could test whether condition-specific M63 growth phenotypes reveal metabolic dependencies that are not captured by a generic essentiality label or by ancestry-contaminated predictive scores.

Most importantly, the Lao/Ying dataset already contains 3,909 Keio deletions measured in both LB and M63, with three biological replicates per strain-medium combination. Their stated contribution is precisely standardized time-resolved growth across rich and minimal media, so simply remeasuring M63 growth or showing that M63 differs from LB is not novel by itself.

1. Most promising genuinely novel question

Provisional question:

Can an independently defined, M63-calibrated metabolic dependency score identify condition-specific growth defects among viable Keio deletions that are not explained by historical Gerdes essentiality labels—and does that relationship survive an ancestry-clean frozen prediction test?

That has a potentially real contribution because it separates three quantities that are currently being conflated:

Gerdes essentiality — an historical label.

M63 growth phenotype — the new experimental outcome.

M63 metabolic dependency prediction — a mechanistic computational prediction.

The scientifically interesting unit is therefore condition-specific quantitative growth impairment, not “essential/nonessential.”

There is already evidence that Keio growth varies by medium and that M63 growth rates have been systematically studied. In fact, a 2020 study analyzed growth rates of the same 3,909 Keio deletions in both LB and M63, explicitly linking deletion growth to gene expression. 
PubMed Central (PMC)
 A 2024 Communications Biology study also examined 114 Keio deletions associated with vitamin metabolism in M63 and explicitly investigated genetic/environmental contributions to growth. 
Nature

So the novelty cannot be:

“Keio deletions have different growth in M63.”

It would have to be something closer to:

“A leakage-controlled M63-specific mechanistic model predicts quantitative growth impairment independently of historical essentiality and independently of the published Lao/Ying M63 phenotypes.”

That is a much more interesting and falsifiable claim.

And even that is provisional novelty, not an exhaustive literature-clearance claim.

The most dangerous issue: ancestry

Your biggest methodological threat isn't the three-plate design. It is label/feature ancestry.

If a model was trained using:

Gerdes labels,

published Keio growth,

features derived from databases that themselves incorporated those phenotypes,

gene annotations generated using the target phenotype,

or previous model predictions that ultimately originated from the same labels,

then calling the resulting score “frozen external prediction” would be misleading.

The test should therefore have an explicit provenance table:

feature → source → publication date → whether target Keio/M63 data contributed → whether model training used it → frozen date/version.

I would regard an unresolved ancestry entry as a HOLD, not a footnote.

The Gerdes failure must remain separate

You are right that:

The failed G2 five-rung Gerdes gate stays failed.

The Keio experiment cannot retroactively make it pass.

At most, you get a new statement:

“The original Gerdes-based claim failed its prespecified gate; a separate experiment tests a different hypothesis concerning quantitative M63 growth.”

That distinction is essential.

A particularly bad analysis would be:

Gerdes failed → Keio agrees/disagrees → therefore Gerdes was rescued.

No. Those are different endpoints and potentially different biological constructs.

3. Endpoint and assay validity

The Lao/Ying dataset is actually useful for defining what needs to be controlled.

They report 3,909 BW25113-derived deletions, M63 and LB, three biological replicates, 96-well plates, and OD600 trajectories at 15-minute intervals. They also explicitly say the three replicates were on separate plates but that well locations remained the same. 
Nature

That last point matters.

Plate replication is not equivalent to completely independent strain randomization.

If the new assay preserves fixed gene-to-well positions, plate effects and position effects can become partially confounded with genotype.

I would freeze:

exact strain identifier;

exact gene identifier;

WT reference;

primary endpoint;

replicate definition;

plate/batch covariates;

stock source;

exclusion rules;

treatment of failed/no-growth cultures;

whether the analysis is strain-level or gene-level;

statistical model;

success criterion.

Primary endpoint

I would favor one prespecified quantitative growth endpoint, with secondary endpoints clearly labeled exploratory.

The source itself emphasizes that growth rate, carrying capacity, and lag are distinct biological quantities. 
Nature

Consequently, “we tested K, r, lag, AUC, maximum OD, and several transformations and found the one that worked” would be a substantial multiplicity problem.

4. The 46 stock exceptions are a major predeclared fork

The source reports that strains without visible overnight growth in the initial stock preparation were subsequently recovered using a different culture procedure, and those strains were explicitly recorded in the deposited stock metadata. 
Nature

That creates an important provenance variable.

You should not decide after seeing M63 outcomes whether those strains are retained.

Predeclare something like:

primary analysis: one fixed treatment of exceptional stocks;

sensitivity analysis: the alternative treatment;

report the number affected;

never choose whichever treatment produces the stronger association.

This is especially important because the “exception” itself is related to growth behavior.

5. FBA is currently not a fair comparator

Your stated local FBA setup—generic glucose bound −10 and open oxygen −1000—is not an M63-calibrated physiological model.

Therefore:

Do not call it an M63 prediction.

It can be a generic baseline, but not a condition-matched mechanistic comparator.

For a legitimate M63 comparison, the model should be frozen with a documented correspondence between the simulated environment and the actual assay environment. Otherwise a failure of FBA could simply mean “the computational environment wasn't M63.”

This is particularly important because the proposed novelty depends on condition-specific metabolic dependency, not generic metabolic essentiality.

The foundational Keio paper established the BW25113 single-gene deletion collection and explicitly preserved two independent mutants per deletion to mitigate handling and secondary-mutation problems.

2020 Keio M63 study

Do genes whose deletion causes little/no growth impairment in rich medium but a reproducible impairment in M63 form a condition-specific dependency class that can be predicted from metabolic network position independently of historical essentiality?
