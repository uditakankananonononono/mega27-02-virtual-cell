# Candidate KT2440 phenotype sources and validation dependence

27 September 2026, updated 1:30 PM IST. Research leads only, no new gene-level phenotype scored, no gate update. The 2010 Molina-Henares et al. Pseudomonas putida KT2440 mutant-library paper (PubMed https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20158506/; DOI 10.1111/j.1462-2920.2010.02166.x) screened minimal medium with glucose. Its abstract reports 48 conditionally essential knockout mutants, half among prior model proposals, and 10 available mutants for proposed model-essential genes that proved nonessential under the tested condition. Those are paper-level findings, not our model's gene-wise validation. Exact nutrient formulation, IDs, knockout coverage, phenotype calls and supplemental files have not been retrieved and matched to our iJN1463 model.

The 2019 Nogales et al. reconstruction paper (https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1574030; DOI 10.1111/1462-2920.14843) reports gene-essentiality predictions compared with a knockout library and Bar-seq data, and lists the 2010 paper among references. Its abstract calls the reconstruction iJN1462; the BiGG snapshot we use is named iJN1463. The live BiGG model page (http://bigg.ucsd.edu/models/iJN1463) displays model ID iJN1463 but exactly 1,462 genes, 2,927 reactions and an October 31, 2019 download update. The locally hashed JSON independently has 1,462 genes and 2,927 reactions. A 2021 still-open BiGG naming issue (https://github.com/SBRG/bigg_models/issues/380) says the published model should be called iJN1462 if this is the same reconstruction. These alignments strongly suggest a naming discrepancy, but a gene/reaction-level provenance comparison to the published archive is still needed before calling them bytewise or functionally identical. This published comparison is a dependency warning: the 2010 phenotypes might have influenced reconstruction, curation or model evaluation, so they are not automatically an untouched independent validation cohort.

The open full text of Nogales et al. (2019/2020 publisher OA copy at https://digital.csic.es/handle/10261/212348; downloaded PDF SHA256 `aeade7be57a9f1e33b13c086cf6a23a5c6127e8732b1e716ed63f188f9ed9e24`) makes the reuse explicit. In its Gene essentiality section, iJN1462 glucose-minimal predictions are evaluated against the existing Molina-Henares et al. (2010) PRCC set: 78 genes were predicted conditionally essential after excluding iLB-essential genes; 47 predicted essential and seven predicted nonessential mutants were available, with 85.2% reported accuracy on the tested calls (Fig. 4, Table S4). It also compared glucose, acetate and p-coumarate BarSeq to the model and tuned a -2.7 fitness cutoff, in part by comparing the BarSeq conditional-essential calls with the PRCC library (37 of 44 overlapping calls); Table 3 reports glucose BarSeq accuracy 91.3% and Matthews correlation 0.434. Those are the published authors' results, not our independently rerun or matched measurements. The supplemental gene-level Tables S4 and S7 were identified but not downloaded, and the glucose BarSeq's contributing experiments remain untraced. Because both candidate phenotype sources were part of the published reconstruction's validation and/or cutoff selection, neither is an untouched independent confirmation of our post-result rescuing calls. A reused-evidence contradiction check may still be useful if explicitly labeled and preregistered before seeing gene-level outcomes.

Publisher supplements are now available from Europe PMC (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7078882/supplementaryFiles). S3 iJN1462 XML SHA256 `0c8113ee6412c21df1afbd0682d1e89e73a515b558a2cab29ee8213bb5afb5f0` and our BiGG iJN1463 JSON SHA256 `8232a1ea39af9b9b0313601a3cefca1028e50e53b2ca2c06002c1a9adf7568bf` share all 1,462 exact gene IDs and an exactly matched 106-metabolite biomass-objective stoichiometry, including the audited MoCo, [2Fe-2S] and biotinyl terms. They are not identical model files: S3 has 2,929 reactions/2,155 metabolites, BiGG has 2,927/2,153; 2,800 reaction IDs overlap, with six stoichiometry and 38 bounds changes among those IDs. The S3 XML had one empty, unreferenced compartment that was removed only in a disposable parsing copy. S4 essentiality SHA256 `73afee37058fab4ef254f3d0acd83fd9eeac9f48939d832a7f94c78b5bd7e7ab` includes all 14 MoCo/suppressive-pair target IDs across its lists; S7 BarSeq SHA256 `3788cb2009b295c54301fa939cb28d107e317b90f1835737d465860b49cca322` includes 11 of these target IDs. Membership in the published model or supplemental tables is not an independent phenotype result, and outcomes have not been scored. The full executable structural diff is `results/postresult_published_ijn1462_lineage.json`, protocol `notes/postresult_published_ijn1462_lineage_audit.md`. This is strong direct model-lineage overlap, while numerical differences prevent claiming complete functional equivalence.

A post-result, separately protocol-locked computational transfer used the same 14 fixed target IDs on both S3 and BiGG under each reconstruction's native bounds and then exact same two-term biomass deletions. Unedited WT maxima differed, 0.615631/h (publisher) versus 0.586118/h (BiGG); the paired MoCo deletion rescued the identical six fixed genes in each model, while the [2Fe-2S]/biotinyl double deletion left the same eight target knockout optima at zero despite viable edited WT. Exact statuses/values and source hashes are in `results/postresult_published_model_counterfactual.json`; method in `notes/postresult_published_model_counterfactual_protocol.md`. This transfer supports a model-family objective-dependence finding while strengthening, rather than removing, the need for condition-matched phenotypes and source independence.

The post-result fixed 14-target lookup in the original published supplement tables (`results/postresult_reused_phenotype_targets.json`) found 11 genes in S7 BarSeq: five of six MoCo targets (the exception PP_3457), and six of eight suppressive-pair targets (PP_0437 and PP_1602 absent). For glucose, acetate and p-coumarate, all 11 present genes are coded `FN` against the published model, and all numerical fitness values exceed the authors' chosen -2.7 essentiality cutoff. The S7 sheet-wide glucose confusion totals (TP 1011, TN 37, FP 13, FN 87) reproduce Table 3. Here `FN` is model-essential versus assay non-essential under the study's own scoring convention, not a negative fitness score and not direct single-mutant survival evidence. S4 contains all 14 IDs in its `Model Genes` list; six of the eight suppressive targets also occur in `Glucose Essential Genes`, but that label is a model-prediction column and must NOT be described as experimental phenotype. PP_1293 and six suppressive genes are listed in the PRCC/model-intersection column, but S4's unlabeled comparison-panel gene-ID columns and color codes were not decoded into observed calls. This is selected-after-results, cutoff-tuned, already published validation data for a closely related model; it is a contradiction clue only, not an untouched independent experiment or a gate pass. The archived supplement source is https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7078882/supplementaryFiles.

The same package's Appendix S1 (`EMI-22-255-s001.docx`, SHA256 `bdb9eaacac1ac4094c857f5b3c00bd33b82c998af35b2e466aa06496803378bf`) gives still stronger evidence against calling the older library independent of the model: Section 5 explicitly says essentiality validation helped increase iJN1462 accuracy and recounts revising B12-related model assumptions after 2010 mutant phenotype disagreements; Section 6 says its in-silico LB medium was defined using the KT2440 2010 conditional-essentiality analysis. S1 Section 9 says the BarSeq essentiality threshold -2.7 was selected by maximizing model/assay Matthews correlation. These are primary-source methods statements, not our reconstruction of the authors' motives. They show outcome-informed model and analysis decisions before our post-result reuse.

The 2024 Bleem et al. RB-TnSeq meta-analysis (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10949508/; DOI 10.1128/msystems.00942-23) aggregates 332 samples from 179 conditions and 4,732 measured genes, including growth on carbon and nitrogen sources. The paper warns of transposon polarity in operons. Its contributing assay overlap with the 2019 reconstruction validation remains untraced. The June 2026 Fitness Browser archive (https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/Archive_of_the_RB-TnSeq_Fitness_Browser_June_2026/32680503) advertises a compressed SQLite database and organism-specific fitness files; we have not retrieved exact KT2440 glucose-condition rows or checked accession-level overlap.

Next action is source archaeology, not scoring: obtain the original source supplements and experiment metadata, establish model-version lineage and whether each assay informed reconstruction, identify source gene IDs and condition-matched glucose medium, then commit a validation protocol before opening outcome rows against the saved rescued-gene list. If independence cannot be established, use these data only as a labeled reused-evidence contradiction check. Preserve original failed G2 and current no-phenotype-validation caveat.
